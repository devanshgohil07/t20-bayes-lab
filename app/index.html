How much of a strike rate is noise?

An IPL auction ranks batters by strike rate. A strike rate is an average over a small number of balls, and such averages move around on their own. Before fitting any model, it is worth seeing how much of that movement is chance.

Where the ±16.7 comes from

A ball off the bat produces 0, 1, 2, 4 or 6 runs, and almost nothing else. Count those across all – deliveries in our window and you get a mean of – runs per ball and a variance of –. On the ×100 scale a strike rate uses, that is a per-ball variance of –, so the standard deviation of an observed strike rate off n balls is σ/√n. Put n = 100 and you get – strike-rate points. This number requires no model; it follows from counting runs.

One batter, one prior, one posterior

This is the Normal–Normal update from Sessions 3–4, with a real player in it. Pick someone, tell the model what you believed about T20 batters before you saw them play, and watch the three curves. The number that decides everything is w: the share of the answer that comes from the player's own record rather than from the population.

prior — what you believed beforehand likelihood — what this player did posterior — what you should believe now

Run the sampler yourself

Every result on this site comes from a Gibbs sampler with six closed-form full conditionals, so no Metropolis step is needed. The same sampler is written here in JavaScript and runs in your browser on the real data. Press run to see four chains, started from different values, converge to the same posterior.

R̂ compares the spread between chains with the spread within them; anything above about 1.01 means the chains have not agreed yet. ESS says how many genuinely independent draws the run is worth. Both are defined exactly as in analysis/diagnostics.py, and the browser sampler reproduces the Python posterior means to within Monte Carlo error.

What a strike rate is worth in each league

A strike rate of 140 in the CPL is not the same as 140 in the IPL. The model estimates one offset per league, a translation between scales, from the batters who appear in more than one of them. Fixing the IPL offset at zero is what makes every θ readable as an expected IPL strike rate.

Posterior distributions of δ. A value of −8 means that this league's strike rates read about eight points low once you put them on the IPL scale.

Who does the translating

Bridge players: the number appearing in both leagues. Every offset is identified by these people and nobody else, so the table is the honest measure of how much to believe the numbers on the left.

a data decision worth recording

Our first run used every men's T20 international in the Cricsheet archive. Those matches involve 105 different national teams, from India and Australia to Estonia, Mongolia and Bhutan. That is not a single scoring environment, and no single offset can describe it. The model returned a shortlist headed by batters from associate nations who had recorded very high strike rates against much weaker bowling. We therefore keep only T20Is in which both sides are ICC Full Members. The bridge to the IPL lost one player out of 107.
